walky talky

Bringing students together through meaningful connections and mental wellness—one step and conversation at a time.

tools

timeline
Mar - Apr 2024 (3 wks.)

introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic reshaped how college students connected, moved, and cared for their mental health. As campuses closed and every day interactions became limited, many student (including myself) experienced firsthand how deeply social connection and physical activity influence mental well-being. Inspired by this realization, I created walky talky, a mobile app designed to help college students improve their well-being and build meaningful connections through something simple, accessible, and human: walking and talking together.

challenge

Although campus life has returned to relative normalcy, many college students continue to experience anxiety, depression, and loneliness. While the pandemic amplified these struggles, research revealed that declining student mental health was a challenge that existed long before COVID-19 and has continued beyond it, highlighting an ongoing need for accessible ways to support student well-being. The challenge then was not simply encouraging students to take time for their mental and physical health. It was creating a meaningful way for students to do both that felt approachable, natural, and safe. Through a combination of literature reviews, quantitative research, and student perspectives exploring the impact of COVID-19, the benefits of walking, and the evolving needs of college students, it became clear that walking serves as a powerful bridge between physical wellness and social interaction.

solution

I designed walky talky, a campus-based mobile app where students can discover walking buddies, coordinate meetups, and build connections one step at a time. Starting from sketches and user flows, I iterated upon different ways to make the experience feel approachable, spontaneous, yet simple. By studying patterns from existing social, fitness, and location-based apps, I created a system that felt fun and organic while remaining purpose-built around the specific goal of fostering conversations and connections through an everyday movement. Designing for connection also required thoughtful consideration of safety, leading me to focus walky talky specifically within college and university communities. In the future, I’d also love to expand walky talky to other demographics such as older adults and/or young working adults, as I've found these two demographics also seek to stay active and socially connected.

result

The project culminated in an interactive Figma prototype and became my first end-to-end UI/UX passion project! Along the way, I strengthened my ability to synthesize qualitative and quantitative research, translate insights into product decisions, and design intuitive mobile interactions. More importantly, it reinforced a lesson that continues to shape my work today: meaningful experiences don't always require complex solutions. Sometimes, it's simply helping people find each other. I am ultimately proud of walky talky and believe that it is something that would truly help bring people together step at a time.
